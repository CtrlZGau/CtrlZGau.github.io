As Software Subsystem Lead at Criss Robotics, I worked on the autonomy stack that helped the team place third in the European Rover Challenge 2025 Remote Category. The remote format made simulation fidelity and operator feedback especially important: the rover needed autonomy that was useful, explainable, and recoverable when the environment did not match the ideal case.

The software stack centered on ROS-based navigation, terrain perception, and multi-sensor fusion. I helped design workflows for mapping the rover's surroundings, estimating traversability, and producing navigation behavior that could be tested repeatedly in simulation before competition runs.

## Contributions

- Developed autonomous navigation workflows for waypoint-driven rover motion.
- Built 3D terrain mapping components for remote environment understanding.
- Integrated multi-sensor fusion pipelines for more reliable state estimation.
- Used Unity-based simulation to test rover behavior, debug failure cases, and support competition operations.

Rover autonomy is a good reminder that robotics software is never only an algorithm. It is also launch files, logs, calibration, visualization, operator trust, and recovery paths. The strongest systems are the ones that make failure easy to inspect.
